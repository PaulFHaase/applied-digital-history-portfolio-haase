### 8

14 says people 38 times, 15 says government 47 times, and 53 says nation 13 times. When switching to relative frequency, it becomes apparent that there are no huge spikes. Document length accounts for most of the "higher" spikes in e.g. 14 and 15.

In this corpus, the relative frequency of government is higher in the Jackson (13) address than in the Wilson (33) address. One explanation is that Jackson was concerned with unity among the (at the time) 24 states of the Union, especially since the US was at peace at the time and could focus on internal affairs. Wilson meanwhile was less concerned with internal politics but with international politics - the US entering WW1 during his term and him trying to form some sort of non-revanchist peace in Europe. It appears unwise to draw long-term conclusions from only comparing two data points in a timeline. In order to further determine if this difference is owed to the two presidents having different goals, it would help to close read a policy paper from both. One might also need secondary opinions from other scholars - judging presidents is something that is often done (policy evaluation, public sentiment analysis).

### 9

"This country, with its institutions, belongs to the people who inhabit it" (19)

Here, "people" appears to refer to the inhabitants of the US. Excluded are (probably) Natives. The "people" appear to own government - trying to appear humble.

"The essential democracy of our Nation and the safety of our people depend not upon the absence of power" (38)

Here, "people" refers to the inhabitants of the US again. A more paternalistic approach can be seen by usage of "our" and "safety", also the sentence tries to evoke a need for law and order ("absence of power"). 

"I do not believe that any of us would exchange places with any other people or any other generation" (44)

Here, "people" refers to other nation's inhabitants and/or other cultures. The sentence tries to positively accentuate the privilege of Americans by contrasting them with the 'Other'.
